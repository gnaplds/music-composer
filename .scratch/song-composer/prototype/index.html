Help & tutorials

Notes are not saved. Reloading or closing this page clears your composition. This prototype uses a temporary synth sound.

Write a phrase
  1. Choose Melody, Chord, or Bass. The amber row is the part used by the piano.
  2. Set the position and note duration, then click piano keys to add notes. Position 0 starts the first measure.
  3. Melody advances the orange cursor to each note’s end. Chord and Bass keep it fixed so you can add notes together. Change this with Advance after entry, or use Next step.

Chord starts with Half notes; Melody and Bass start with Quarter notes. Default duration beside the piano and Note duration below the timeline control the same setting, remembered separately for each part.

Use dotted values, Quarter triplet, or Eighth triplet from the duration menu. Three quarter triplets fill two quarter-note beats. The piano auditions only during playback; pause to write.

Select, move, resize, and delete notes
  • Click a note to edit its pitch, start, or duration in the side panel.
  • Left-drag the body to move in time; drag the right edge to resize. Snap uses Quarter, Eighth, Quarter triplet, or Eighth triplet.
  • Ctrl/Cmd-click or Shift-click toggles notes in a group, including across parts. Moving a group keeps its spacing; resizing changes every duration by the same amount.
  • Right-click a timeline note to delete it. Right-click a piano key to delete that pitch’s note active at the cursor in the selected part.
  • Escape cancels a drag. Undo and Redo restore musical edits; a completed group drag is one edit.

Pitch, part, and duration stay fixed when moving. Resize snaps from the note’s start. Same-pitch notes cannot overlap within one part, but may meet at an end/start boundary. Invalid group edits are rejected together.

Notes can sustain across boxes and measures. Editing a held note changes its complete span. Moving or extending notes past the end adds measures.

Adjust the workspace

Drag the handle above the piano up or down to change the key height. When the handle has keyboard focus, use Up/Down for small changes or Home/End for the limits. Escape cancels an unfinished drag. Height is limited to keep the timeline usable and lasts for this page session. Portrait phone keys start taller than landscape keys.

Hide parts hides all Melody, Chord, and Bass rows while keeping chord labels and the measure ruler visible. The notes still play. Show parts restores the rows and their collapse settings. On phones, this button is in Tools. Key labels start off; turn them on with Key labels: Off.

Keys for new passages

Next step and automatic advance give an empty destination box the tonic and major/minor mode of the collection active there. Piano entry also applies this default to empty or new boxes reached by the new note. A box with any sounding note, including one held from an earlier box, keeps its key. Merely seeking or changing the collection does not change keys. You can still set a different key explicitly.

Advancing beyond the song does not add measures; the key is assigned when a note creates the new boxes. Key changes stay within those boxes. Undo reverses a Next step key change; key defaults during piano entry are undone together with that note.

Compose on a phone

Write in portrait with one piano octave and taller keys. Landscape offers two octaves. Choose a part and duration above the piano. Use the octave buttons for other pitches. Tap a row’s arrow to expand or collapse it.

Swipe the timeline to scroll, including from notes. Tap a note to select it without moving the orange cursor. Select → Notes or Boxes lets taps add or remove items; Clear empties the selection. Note selections survive part changes.

Choose Move or Resize, then drag a selected note to preview the group edit. Release applies one edit and returns to scrolling. Cancel or an interrupted touch discards the preview. Delete shows the note count; one Undo restores the whole group. A long press never deletes music.

Tap a beat or empty row, or drag the orange handle, to set the cursor. Seeking pauses playback. Tools contains harmony, context, loop, view, and display settings. Both orientations support writing and playback. Rotating preserves the current scratch composition, but reloading still clears it.

Navigate the timeline

Click a row to choose a part and position. Drag the orange cursor to seek silently: it snaps to nearby note starts and ends, then the selected rhythm grid. The piano highlights all notes active at that position.

Hold the right mouse button and drag to pan, including from a note. A drag does not delete notes; Escape restores the previous view. Use Zoom for short passages. Note entry and navigation smoothly center the cursor; manual panning remains free until the next edit.

Focus the cursor handle and use Left/Right arrows to move by Snap, or Home/End to reach the song boundaries. Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes; Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z redoes. Collapse a part with its row button; its notes still play.

Chord boxes, keys, and time signatures

Boxes organize time; their notes remain independent. Split or merge a box at the cursor without moving notes.

Click a chord label to select one box, Ctrl/Cmd-click to toggle boxes, or Shift-click to select a range. Set key changes only selected boxes and restores the surrounding keys. With no boxes selected, it applies from the cursor until the next key marker.

Key, harmonic collection, and meter are separate settings. Changing key never transposes notes. A meter change starts a new measure at the cursor and reorganizes later measures without moving written notes. Key labels toggles the local key beneath each chord name.

Playback, loops, and volume

Play starts from the cursor; Pause stops sound, and Play resumes. Choosing a new position sets the next start. Loop measures repeats the selected range and restarts held notes at its beginning. The playing box is highlighted, and the timeline follows ahead to show the next chord.

Tempo is quarter-note BPM. Metronome clicks quarters in 2/4, 3/4, and 4/4; it clicks two dotted quarters in 6/8. The first pulse of each measure is accented. There is no count-in or swing yet.

Each part has its own volume slider in 5% steps. Part volume does not change the metronome. Notes held at a playback start sound for their remaining duration.

Piano colors and symbols

● identifies the sixth-chord tones, ◆ the diminished-chord tones, and ○ notes outside the selected collection. The piano starts with plain white and black keys. Switch on Note names or Symbols to show labels; Symbols uses the family colors. There are no color strips under the keys. C major sixth-diminished combines C–E–G–A with D–F–A♭–B. Minor sixth-diminished lowers the sixth chord’s third.

The active collection follows the editing cursor while stopped and the playhead while playing. Sounding-note highlights are separate from these family colors. The octave buttons change the visible register. Dark mode, beside New composition, changes the editor and help panel while keeping the piano white and black. These display choices last for the page session and do not change your notes.

Chord names and Roman numerals

Degrees use the tonic’s major scale in both modes. Inversion figures are stacked: triads use 6 or 6 over 4; sevenths use 6 over 5, 4 over 3, or 4 over 2. A slash before a Roman numeral still means “of,” as in V/IV. (add6) means an added sixth; sixth and suspended chords name their bass explicitly. V/x, subV/x, and vii°/x offer dominant, tritone-substitute, and leading-tone readings. A mode name records a possible borrowing source. Choose the reading you intend; notes, key, and colors stay unchanged. Recognition covers triads, sus2/sus4, sixths, and sevenths in any register. Ninths and other extended sets remain playable with custom labels.

Current harmony explanation

Names use the sounding Bass and Chord notes, including held notes; Melody is excluded. Choose an alternative or a custom label without changing the notes. The actual lowest note determines the inversion. Review warnings remain visible if a chosen interpretation no longer fits.

Context markers

This prototype recognizes exact matches from the initial vocabulary. Incomplete matches show their notes; missing-note suggestions are deferred. Pitch labels use practical flat names here. The final spelling rules remain in the specification.

Prototype limits

This version keeps a scratch composition in memory only. Saving, MIDI export/import, live recording, final piano sound, and the full voicing toolbox are not available. The maximum song length is 256 quarter notes. Undo history lasts for this page session.

Recognition covers major, minor, diminished, and augmented triads, sus2/sus4, sixths, and sevenths. Extended or incomplete sets remain playable without a guaranteed name. Pitch spelling uses practical piano-key names. Opening help does not change your music; guided examples ask before replacing an existing phrase.
